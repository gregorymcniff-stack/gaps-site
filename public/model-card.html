Model details

ModelsSky Friction Index: chance of 3+ new US ground stops or ground delay programs (any airport) in the next 3 hours.
Per-airport outlooks: chance of a new ground stop or ground delay program at each of 15 US hubs in the next 3 hours.
TypeLogistic regression on standardised inputs (one model for the index; one pooled model for the 15 airports), with the intercept re-centred on the most recent 30 days.

Intended use

For: situational awareness by aviation enthusiasts, travellers, journalists, data scientists and aviation professionals; seeing whether US airports are heading into a rough few hours, and where.

Not for: flight planning, dispatch, air traffic or any operational or safety decision; predicting individual flights; airports outside the US; commercial forecasting without checking the licences of the underlying data.

Training and evaluation data

Target eventsNew US ground stops and ground delay programs from the FAA ATCSCC Advisory Database (cancellations, proposals, revisions, extensions and non-US facilities excluded): 2,788 programs. Download.
WeatherOpen-Meteo historical forecasts (hourly weather code, gusts, visibility, low cloud) at 15 US hubs. The live model uses the same Open-Meteo forecast product.

SplitTime-ordered: fitted on the first two-thirds, scored on the final third (six weeks), never seen during fitting. The production models are then refitted on the whole period.
Not usedAircraft counts (no free historical archive), weather at 8 world airports and jet-stream winds (no predictive value in testing), airport forecasts (TAFs; no free historical archive was available).

Metrics and results

Main metric: AUC on the held-out weeks: how often an hour followed by the event is ranked above one that was not (0.5 = coin flip, 1.0 = perfect). Every model is compared with simpler rivals.

Limitations and risks

  • Seasonal drift. Trained mostly on summer storm season; on the autumn test weeks the higher estimates ran too high (for example, the index said about 61% when the rate was about 41%). Mitigation: re-centring on the last 30 days. The re-centred model has not yet been tested on new data.
  • No winter. Four months of data (July–November). Snow, de-icing and winter storms are not represented.
  • Leans on persistence. Recent programs are a strong input, so the outlooks are better at recognising a bad stretch under way than at warning before the first program.
  • Small airports in the test. DFW, ATL, IAH, CLT and LAX had fewer than 15 programs in the test weeks and cannot be judged on their own.
  • Coarse target. A short ground stop and an all-day delay program count the same.
  • Data dependencies. FAA advisories are read from a public web page (not an official API); weather and aircraft come from free services with usage limits. When an input is missing, the outlook is not shown rather than estimated.
  • US only. No free equivalent of the FAA records exists for Europe or elsewhere.

Maintenance

  • Inputs and outlooks are stored every 10 minutes (open data), so live performance can be scored against what actually happened.
  • Planned: a live track record on the dashboard, and monthly refits with time-series cross-validation and uncertainty intervals.
  • Monitoring: /api/health is checked every 5 minutes by an external service.
