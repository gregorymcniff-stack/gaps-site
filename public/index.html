      How GAPS works, in three steps

      What GAPS tells you

      1

      The question

      Will US air travel have a rough next 3 hours? We count it as rough when the FAA has to start 3 or more new ground stops or delay programs: its tools for holding or slowing flights when an airport can't cope. That happens in about 1 hour in 5.

      2

      The clues

      Is it already happening? New FAA programs in the last few hours. Is bad weather coming to major US airports? Is it a busy time of day? Evenings are worst.

      3

      The answer

      A score from 1 to 10, where 5 is average. Each clue gets its own 1–10 score, and the weighted average is the final score. Tested on 4 months of FAA records. How we know →

      About GAPS

      GAPS watches the airline network and estimates how likely US airports are to hit a bad stretch of disruption in the next few hours. Every number on this page comes from a live public feed and shows its age. When a feed is down, its panel says so; nothing is filled in.

      The GAPS score is built from the three things that predicted disruption in two years of FAA records (November 2024–November 2026, including two winters): programs that have just started, forecast weather at major US hubs, and the time of day. An earlier version, built from world traffic, weather at 8 world airports and jet-stream winds, did no better than a coin flip in the same test and was replaced.

      Who's behind it: GAPS is an independent hobbyist project, free to use, with no ads and no tracking. It has no affiliation to any airline, government agency or aviation authority. It is not a safety system and does not predict individual flights. Questions or ideas? Send feedback.
